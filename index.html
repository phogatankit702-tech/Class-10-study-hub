📚 Class 10 Science Chapter Notes

Chapter 1: Chemical Reactions and Equations
  • Chemical Reaction: Process where reactants transform into new products with different properties.
  • Balanced Equation: Follows the Law of Conservation of Mass (equal number of atoms on both sides).
  • Reaction Types: Combination, Decomposition, Displacement, Double Displacement, Exothermic, Endothermic, and Redox (Oxidation & Reduction).
Chapter 2: Acids, Bases and Salts
  • Acids & Bases: Acids release $H^+$ ions (pH < 7); Bases release $OH^-$ ions (pH > 7).
  • Neutralization: $Acid + Base \rightarrow Salt + Water$.
  • Important Compounds: Bleaching Powder ($CaOCl_2$), Baking Soda ($NaHCO_3$), Washing Soda ($Na_2CO_3 \cdot 10H_2O$), Plaster of Paris ($CaSO_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}H_2O$).
Chapter 3: Metals and Non-Metals
  • Properties: Metals are malleable, ductile, good conductors; Non-metals are non-ductile and dull.
  • Reactivity Series: Arrangement of metals in decreasing reactivity order.
  • Ionic Compounds: Formed by electron transfer between metals and non-metals; high melting/boiling points.
Chapter 4: Carbon and Its Compounds
  • Covalent Bonding: Sharing of electron pairs between atoms; tetravalency and catenation properties.
  • Hydrocarbons: Alkanes ($C_nH_{2n+2}$), Alkenes ($C_nH_{2n}$), Alkynes ($C_nH_{2n-2}$).
  • Soaps & Detergents: Soap molecules form micelles to trap dirt and grease.
Chapter 5: Life Processes
  • Nutrition: Autotrophic (photosynthesis in plants) and Heterotrophic (holozoic, saprophytic, parasitic).
  • Respiration: Aerobic (in mitochondria, yields 38 ATP) and Anaerobic (in cytoplasm/yeast/muscles).
  • Transport & Excretion: Double circulation in human heart; urine filtration by nephrons in kidneys.
Chapter 6: Control and Coordination
  • Nervous System: Neurons transmit impulses via electrical and chemical signals (synapse).
  • Plant Hormones: Auxins, Gibberellins, Cytokinins (growth promoters); Abscisic acid (growth inhibitor).
  • Endocrine System: Glands secrete hormones (e.g., Insulin from pancreas, Thyroxine from thyroid).
Chapter 7: How Do Organisms Reproduce?
  • Asexual Reproduction: Binary fission, fragmentation, regeneration, budding, spore formation, vegetative propagation.
  • Sexual Reproduction: Male and female gametes fuse (fertilization) to form a zygote.
  • Reproductive Health: Contraceptive methods (barrier, chemical, surgical) and prevention of STDs.
Chapter 8: Heredity
  • Mendel's Laws: Monohybrid cross (Phenotypic ratio 3:1) and Dihybrid cross (9:3:3:1 ratio).
  • Inheritance: Dominant vs Recessive traits.
  • Sex Determination: Human females have $XX$ chromosomes; males have $XY$ chromosomes.
Chapter 9: Light - Reflection and Refraction
  • Mirror Formula: $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u}$ (Concave mirror forms real/virtual images).
  • Lens Formula: $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u}$.
  • Power of Lens: $P = \frac{1}{f \text{ (in meters)}}$, measured in Dioptres ($D$).
Chapter 10: Human Eye and Colourful World
  • Defects of Vision: Myopia (corrected by Concave lens), Hypermetropia (corrected by Convex lens).
  • Prism Dispersion: Splitting of white light into VIBGYOR spectrum.
  • Atmospheric Phenomena: Twinkling of stars (refraction), Red sunset & Blue sky (Rayleigh scattering).
Chapter 11: Electricity
  • Ohm's Law: $V = IR$ at constant temperature.
  • Resistors: Series combination ($R_s = R_1 + R_2 + R_3$); Parallel combination ($\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3}$).
  • Heating Effect: Joule's law of heating ($H = I^2 Rt$).
Chapter 12: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current
  • Magnetic Field: Field lines form closed loops; strongest near magnetic poles.
  • Right-Hand Thumb Rule: Gives direction of magnetic field around a current-carrying wire.
  • Fleming's Left-Hand Rule: Determines force direction on a conductor in a magnetic field.
Chapter 13: Our Environment
  • Food Chain & Web: Energy flow is unidirectional.
  • 10% Law: Only 10% energy transfers to the next trophic level.
  • Environmental Issues: Ozone layer depletion ($CFCs$) and biological magnification.

📝 15 Important Practice MCQs

Q1. $Fe_2O_3 + 2Al \rightarrow Al_2O_3 + 2Fe$ is an example of a:
(a) Combination reaction
(b) Double displacement reaction
(c) Decomposition reaction
(d) Displacement reaction
Show Answer

Answer: (d) Displacement reaction. Highly reactive Aluminum displaces Iron.

Q2. Which chemical compound is used for softening hard water?
(a) Baking Soda
(b) Washing Soda ($Na_2CO_3 \cdot 10H_2O$)
(c) Bleaching Powder
(d) Plaster of Paris
Show Answer

Answer: (b) Washing Soda. It removes permanent hardness of water.

Q3. Which non-metal is liquid at room temperature?
(a) Iodine
(b) Carbon
(c) Bromine
(d) Sulfur
Show Answer

Answer: (c) Bromine. It is the only liquid non-metal.

Q4. What is the general formula for Alkanes?
(a) $C_nH_{2n}$
(b) $C_nH_{2n+2}$
(c) $C_nH_{2n-2}$
(d) $C_nH_n$
Show Answer

Answer: (b) $C_nH_{2n+2}$. Represents saturated hydrocarbons.

Q5. The breakdown of pyruvate into carbon dioxide, water, and energy takes place in:
(a) Cytoplasm
(b) Mitochondria
(c) Chloroplast
(d) Nucleus
Show Answer

Answer: (b) Mitochondria. This is the site for aerobic respiration.

Q6. Which plant hormone inhibits growth and causes wilting of leaves?
(a) Auxin
(b) Cytokinin
(c) Abscisic Acid
(d) Gibberellin
Show Answer

Answer: (c) Abscisic Acid. It promotes leaf detachment and wilting.

Q7. The male reproductive part of a flower (Stamen) consists of:
(a) Style and Stigma
(b) Anther and Filament
(c) Ovary and Ovule
(d) Sepals and Petals
Show Answer

Answer: (b) Anther and Filament. They make up the stamen.

Q8. What is the phenotypic ratio of a Monohybrid cross in F2 generation?
(a) 1:2:1
(b) 3:1
(c) 9:3:3:1
(d) 2:1
Show Answer

Answer: (b) 3:1. 3 dominant to 1 recessive phenotype.

Q9. Focal length of a convex lens is $+50\text{ cm}$. Its power is:
(a) $+2\text{ D}$
(b) $-2\text{ D}$
(c) $+0.02\text{ D}$
(d) $+5\text{ D}$
Show Answer

Answer: (a) $+2\text{ D}$. $P = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{0.5\text{ m}} = +2\text{ D}$.

Q10. Myopia (near-sightedness) is corrected using a:
(a) Convex lens
(b) Concave lens
(c) Bifocal lens
(d) Cylindrical lens
Show Answer

Answer: (b) Concave lens. Diverges light to focus on the retina.

Q11. The SI unit of electric current is:
(a) Ohm
(b) Volt
(c) Ampere
(d) Joule
Show Answer

Answer: (c) Ampere. Unit of rate of flow of electric charge.

Q12. What happens to resistance if the length of a conductor is doubled?
(a) Halved
(b) Doubled
(c) Remains same
(d) Four times
Show Answer

Answer: (b) Doubled. Resistance is directly proportional to length ($R \propto L$).

Q13. Which rule is used to find the direction of force on a current-carrying conductor?
(a) Right-Hand Thumb Rule
(b) Fleming's Left-Hand Rule
(c) Fleming's Right-Hand Rule
(d) Ohm's Law
Show Answer

Answer: (b) Fleming's Left-Hand Rule. Used for motors and conductors in magnetic fields.

Q14. How much energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next?
(a) 10%
(b) 1%
(c) 50%
(d) 100%
Show Answer

Answer: (a) 10%. Based on Lindeman's 10% law of energy transfer.

Q15. Depletion of the ozone layer is mainly caused by:
(a) Methane
(b) Carbon Dioxide
(c) Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
(d) Nitrogen Oxide
Show Answer

Answer: (c) Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs). They release chlorine radicals that destroy ozone.
